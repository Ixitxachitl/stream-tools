Stunt Wheel

A tiny pixel-art wheel races along the bottom of your stream on a track that never ends: ramps, boost pads and loop-the-loops, pinball bumpers and flippers, slot machines and roulette wheels. When people chat, they get a wheel of their own in their chat color: it races in, parks next to yours to say its piece in a pixel speech bubble, then zooms off (unless it hits a die on the way).

Setup guide

Get it on stream

  1. Add a Browser source In OBS click + under Sources → Browser. Paste https://race.ixitxachitl.com/ as the URL and set it to your full canvas size, usually 1920 × 1080. The track runs along the bottom edge, and the background is transparent automatically.
  2. Open the settings Right-click the source → Interact. Move your mouse over the overlay and click the orange gear in the bottom-right corner.
  3. Connect your chat (optional) Type your Twitch and/or Kick channel name, and add TikTok or YouTube if you stream there. Without chat, the wheel just keeps racing on its own.
  4. Save & apply Press Save & apply and close the Interact window. Your settings are saved inside this browser source. Press Test chat first if you want to see a chat wheel roll in.
The settings window shows on stream while it's open. Set things up before you go live, or hide the source while you tweak.

Chat sources

Twitch & Kick

Just type the channel name. Chat is read anonymously, so there's no login and no bot account needed. Emotes show up as pictures in the bubbles, and a timeout or ban takes that chatter's wheel off the track.

TikTok

TikTok needs a bridge app running on your PC. If you use TikFinity, just type 1 in the TikTok box. Otherwise, paste your bridge's ws://… address.

YouTube

Paste your live stream's video ID or link, plus a YouTube Data API v3 key from the Google Cloud Console. You can also give a bridge ws://… address instead.

What you'll see

  • Hills, bumps, striped ramps over gaps and full loop-the-loops. The track builds itself as it goes, so no two runs are the same, and it's built so your wheel always makes it on its own: a blue boost pad turns up before any gap or loop that needs the speed, and a green spring before every die or chip stack.
  • Pinball pieces: pop bumpers that kick wheels into the air for points, flippers hinged on the edge of a gap that swat them across, and drop targets to knock flat for a bonus.
  • Casino pieces: slot machines the track runs under (now and then one hits 7-7-7 and pays out a JACKPOT), loops with a roulette wheel spinning inside, and dice and chip stacks to spring over. Your wheel always makes it; chat wheels sometimes miss the spring, and go BOOM.
  • Roller-coaster runs: a chain lift hauls the wheels up, then dips and a drop, sometimes straight into a loop on the drop's speed alone. Domino chains topple ahead of the first wheel to reach them.
  • Behind it all, an old-time circus (striped tents, game booths, clowns, people and a circus train with cage wagons) in front of a Vegas skyline. Dice, poker chips, coins, card suits and stars drift past too.
  • Every chatter gets a wheel in their chat color. It races in from the left, parks near the middle while its bubble is up, then races off to the right.
  • Your own messages come from the main wheel, in the middle, and it takes on your chat color (it remembers it, too).
  • Bubbles stay put while their wheel jumps and loops underneath them, so they're easy to read.
  • Busy chat waits its turn: only a few wheels are on the track at once, and the next one rolls in when there's room. Common bots are ignored.
